### 3. Business Model & Economic Moat

**Revenue Architecture (How It Makes Money):**

Rocket Lab operates a **vertically integrated, full-stack space infrastructure** business across two segments:

1. **Launch Services (~32% of TTM Revenue):** Electron — the world's second most frequently launched orbital rocket. Capable of 52 launches per year at current factory design. At ~$8–10M per launch, Electron targets the small satellite/rideshare market. HASTE (Hypersonic Accelerator Suborbital Test Electron) is a growing defense subvariant. Launches on manifest: 70+.

2. **Space Systems (~68% of TTM Revenue):** The higher-margin, faster-growing segment, including:
   - *Spacecraft Components:* Solar panels, reaction wheels, star trackers, radios, separation systems — sold to OEMs and satellite manufacturers globally. These are recurring, high-margin consumable-like revenues with meaningful switching costs.
   - *Spacecraft Builds:* Full turn-key satellite manufacturing (e.g., the SDA Tranche 3 PWSA constellation — 18 satellites under the $816M Space Force prime contract awarded December 2025).
   - *Mynaric acquisition (completed April 14, 2026):* Laser optical communication terminals, adding European access and in-house OISL capability for LEO constellations. Deal valued at $155.3M (nominal cash + 2,277,002 RKLB shares).

3. **Neutron (Upcoming — Q4 2026 first launch):** A reusable medium-lift vehicle (~13,000 kg to LEO), targeting the Falcon 9 medium payload class. Five commercial contracts signed. Largest single contract in company history was a bulk Neutron + Electron launch deal with a confidential customer, bringing manifest to 70+ missions.

**Competitive Advantage (Economic Moat Analysis):**

*Moat Rating: Moderate — Widening*

- **High Switching Costs (Space Systems):** Satellite manufacturers designing Rocket Lab reaction wheels, star trackers, and solar panels into their spacecraft face significant recertification costs to switch suppliers. Design-in cycles are multi-year.
- **Proprietary Technology:** Rutherford engine (electric pump-fed, largely 3D-printed) and Archimedes engine (Neutron) represent significant IP. Phoenix infrared payload (missile-warning sensor) awarded under the SDA contract is a national security capability with meaningful barriers.
- **Track Record & Launch Reliability:** 50+ successful Electron launches, 100% mission success rate in 2025. In launch services, reliability is the primary purchasing criterion — each failed launch is potentially a total loss of a $200M+ satellite. Track record is a genuine moat against new entrants.
- **Vertical Integration:** Controlling the full stack — components, bus, launch vehicle — creates cost advantages and a closed-loop customer value proposition competitors cannot easily replicate.
- **Defense Prime Status:** The SDA PWSA prime contract elevates RKLB into the small circle of U.S. national security space primes alongside Northrop Grumman, L3Harris, and Boeing. Certification to this tier is a years-long process.

*What the Market Is Missing (Core Thesis):*

The market is pricing RKLB primarily as a launch company. It is actually becoming a national security space prime contractor. The Space Systems segment ($136.7M in Q1 alone, +57% YoY) is larger, faster-growing, and structurally higher-margin than Launch. The $816M SDA prime contract is not a project — it is a proof-of-concept for RKLB as a sovereign Western alternative to Boeing/Northrop on constellation programs. If Neutron works, RKLB becomes the only Western company offering integrated spacecraft + medium-lift at scale. That value is not fully captured in a simple revenue multiple analysis.

### 4. Market Opportunity & Addressable Market (TAM / SAM / SOM)

- **TAM (Total Space Economy):** $1.0–1.8 Trillion by 2035 (Morgan Stanley/Goldman; multiple industry sources). The global space launch services market specifically is $14.9B (2023) growing to $41.3B by 2030 at a 14.6% CAGR (Grand View Research, 2024).
- **SAM (RKLB's Addressable Segments):**
  - *Small Launch (Electron):* ~$3–5B globally by 2030, with RKLB holding a dominant Western position.
  - *Medium Launch (Neutron):* ~$8–12B annually by 2030 (Boeing/SpaceX Falcon 9 class), representing a step-change in TAM access.
  - *Spacecraft Components & Buses:* ~$15B+ by 2030, driven by LEO megaconstellations.
  - *National Security Space (Systems):* $20B+ annually in U.S. DoD satellite procurement. RKLB is now an approved prime.
  - *Aggregate SAM estimate:* $25–35B by 2028 across all segments.
- **SOM (5-Year Realistic Capture):** At $1.5–2.0B revenue by 2028 (base case trajectory), RKLB captures approximately 5–8% of its SAM — achievable, but contingent on Neutron performing.

5. **DOD/National Security Spending:** Despite ongoing DoD budget pressures, proliferated LEO architectures (PWSA, etc.) remain a bipartisan priority. RKLB is positioned as an essential sovereign supplier.

**Negative Catalysts (Headwinds to Watch):**

1. **Neutron Delay or First-Flight Failure:** Already delayed once (from late 2025 to Q4 2026), a second delay would severely damage management credibility and compress the valuation multiple. A failed first launch could send the stock down 30–50%.
2. **DoD Budget Pressure:** ~80% of RKLB's revenue is defense-adjacent. If the DoD implements deeper cuts to space programs beyond the flagged 8% annual reduction, near-term revenue risk increases.
3. **Gross Margin Compression During SDA Ramp:** Q2 guidance signals margin compression. If program costs overrun, GAAP gross margin could fall below 30%, triggering analyst estimate resets.
4. **Capital Dilution Risk:** FCF is -$316M TTM and Neutron capex will accelerate through 2026. If cash burn exceeds $400M in FY2026, a secondary offering at current elevated multiples would be heavily dilutive on a per-share basis despite the stock price.
5. **SpaceX Competition:** If SpaceX accelerates dedicated small-sat Falcon 9 rideshare pricing or Starship's sub-orbital capabilities expand, Electron's competitive position narrows.

### 7. Key Investment Risks & Counter-Thesis

**Company-Specific Risks:**

- **Neutron Execution Risk:** This is the primary binary risk event. Neutron has already been delayed once. A 13,000 kg-class reusable vehicle is an extraordinarily complex engineering undertaking. SpaceX took years of failures before Falcon 9 became reliable. A first-flight anomaly is a high-probability outcome statistically, and the stock's current valuation may already partially price Neutron success.
- **Cash Burn Trajectory:** FCF burn of -$316M TTM, with Neutron CapEx accelerating. If RKLB burns $350–400M in FY2026, the $1.48B cash balance contracts meaningfully. A secondary equity offering (even at $80/share) would be 10–15% dilutive.
- **SBC & Dilution:** Shares outstanding grew approximately 11% year-over-year (from ~520M to 578.87M). SBC as a percent of revenue remains elevated (~15–17% estimate). Dilution is a structural headwind at current share counts.
- **Program Cost Overruns (SDA Contract):** Fixed-price government contracts carry cost overrun risk. If the 18-satellite PWSA build exceeds cost assumptions, RKLB absorbs the delta — a risk that has damaged many aerospace primes historically.
- **Customer Concentration:** Defense customers represent ~80% of revenue. Any single large program slippage — particularly the SDA constellation — would materially impact near-term numbers.

**Macroeconomic & Systematic Risks:**

- **Interest Rate / Risk-Off Environment:** RKLB trades at 91x trailing revenue. A sustained increase in long-term rates or a broad-market risk-off rotation would compress high-multiple growth stocks disproportionately. RKLB's intrinsic value is extremely duration-sensitive.
- **Geopolitical Risk:** While defense spending is a tailwind, geopolitical events that alter U.S. DoD priority structures (e.g., a major reshuffling of space vs. conventional weapons spending) could affect the SDA pipeline.
- **SpaceX Starship Commercialization:** If SpaceX brings Starship to commercial medium-lift at sub-$20M per launch economics faster than Neutron reaches operational maturity, the Neutron business case weakens substantially.
- **Regulatory / FCC / ITAR Risk:** As a U.S. national security space contractor, RKLB's international commercial revenue (New Zealand launch site, European customers via Mynaric) carries ITAR compliance exposure.
