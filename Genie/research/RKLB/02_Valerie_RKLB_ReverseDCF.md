## Section 1 — Reverse DCF Summary: What Must Rocket Lab Become?

At an EV of ~$60.74 billion against TTM revenue of $679.6M, the market is not pricing what RKLB is today. It is pricing what RKLB must be in 7–10 years. The implied business outcomes are as follows:

- **Revenue Scale:** RKLB must reach **$4.0–6.5 billion in revenue** depending on the FCF margin scenario assumed at terminal value. This represents a **6x–10x revenue increase** from today's TTM.
- **Market Position:** RKLB must become a full-scale national security space prime contractor alongside Northrop Grumman and L3Harris, commanding significant Neutron launch cadence (30+ per year) while maintaining Electron volume.
- **Geographic Scale:** Domestic (U.S. defense ~80%) + Commercial International. Neutron enables launch from Wallops (VA) and potentially New Zealand — two sovereign-access launch sites.
- **Margin Profile:** Must achieve 15–25% FCF margins at steady state (currently deeply negative). This implies substantial operating leverage over a $4B+ revenue base — achievable, but requires Neutron becoming consistently profitable, Space Systems reaching scale, and SBC normalization.
- **Competitive Position:** Must sustain dominant Western small-launch monopoly (Electron) while Neutron captures a meaningful share (~5–10%) of the Falcon 9-class medium-lift market — all while SpaceX continues to scale.
- **Profitability Timeline:** The market is pricing RKLB reaching FCF breakeven by approximately 2028–2029, turning meaningfully FCF positive by 2030–2032. Any structural delay to this path materially compresses the implied value.
- **Capital Discipline:** Must achieve the above without excessive further share dilution — i.e., shares outstanding must not grow beyond 650–700M while achieving scale, or per-share EV accretes only marginally.
- **Defense Prime Status:** The SDA PWSA contract must be executed flawlessly and followed by subsequent constellation prime awards ($500M–$1B scale) to maintain defense prime credentials.
- **Neutron Commercialization:** At least 15–20 Neutron launches per year by 2030 at $50M+ ASP would contribute $750M–$1B of launch revenue alone — a necessary building block for the base case revenue target.
- **M&A Integration (Mynaric):** Laser comms terminals must be successfully scaled into RKLB's product family, expanding addressable per-satellite content value.

*Every bullet above is mathematically necessary to justify the current EV. None are guaranteed.*

## Section 12 — Sanity Check

**Are the implied terminal outcomes achievable?**

**Assessment: Very Difficult**

Primary bottlenecks ranked by criticality:

1. **Execution:** Neutron must work. First flight is Q4 2026. Historical base rate for new rocket vehicles achieving commercial viability on first attempt is low. Even SpaceX failed multiple times before Falcon 1 succeeded.

2. **Capital:** Neutron development + SDA satellite production simultaneously + Mynaric integration = multi-front capital deployment. FCF is deeply negative. Capital constraints could force sub-optimal decisions.

3. **Competition:** SpaceX is not standing still. Starship cadence improvements make rideshare economics increasingly compelling. RKLB must execute Neutron before Starship commoditizes the medium-lift market.

4. **Regulation:** National security space prime status requires ongoing FCC, ITAR, and export compliance across New Zealand, European (Mynaric), and U.S. operations. Any violation carries catastrophic contract risk.

5. **Pricing:** Government contracts are won on capability and price. SpaceX's cost structure (massively scale-advantaged) creates pricing pressure that RKLB cannot fully escape. Neutron's reusability is necessary — not sufficient — to compete on margin.

**Conclusion:** The math requires RKLB to become one of the top 5–10 largest defense aerospace companies in the world within 15–20 years. This is a **Very Difficult** outcome — not impossible given the structural tailwinds, but requiring extraordinary execution across every segment simultaneously.
