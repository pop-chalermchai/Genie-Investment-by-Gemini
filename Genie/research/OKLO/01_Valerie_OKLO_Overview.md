### 3. Business Model & Economic Moat

**Revenue Architecture (How it will make money):**

Oklo's business model is fundamentally that of a nuclear power plant developer, owner, and operator — not a pure technology licensor. The company designs, builds, finances, and operates its own reactor fleet, then sells electricity via long-term PPAs (Power Purchase Agreements) priced by the MWh to anchor customers, primarily hyperscale data center operators.

- **Primary Revenue Stream (Target 2027–2028+):** Power sales via PPA from Aurora reactor fleet. The Aurora powerhouse is a liquid-metal-cooled, metallic-fuel fast reactor scaled from 15 MWe to 75 MWe per unit. PPAs with hyperscalers are expected to be priced in the $100–180/MWh range for first-of-a-kind (FOAK) units — a substantial premium to grid rates, justified by the co-location, carbon-free, and firm-power value propositions for AI data centers.
- **Near-Term Revenue (2026):** Atomic Alchemy subsidiary — an acquired radioisotope producer targeting criticality at its Groves reactor in Idaho by July 2026. NRC materials license granted in March 2026. Revenue from isotope production (Ra-226, Co-60, Am-241 and eventually medical isotopes) is immaterial in the near term but represents the only path to recognizing any revenue in 2026. Management guided for first isotope revenues in H1 2026.
- **Future Revenue Stream (Post-2030):** Fuel recycling. Oklo has demonstrated end-to-end advanced fuel recycling (electrochemical process) with Argonne National Laboratory. Used nuclear fuel is ~90% residual energy; recycled fuel represents a structural cost advantage and potential third-party fuel supply revenue.

**Competitive Advantage (Economic Moat Analysis):**

Oklo's moat is **Moderate** but asymmetric in opportunity scale. The moat is not yet validated commercially — it is structural and regulatory in nature:

1. **Proprietary Fast Reactor Technology:** The Aurora powerhouse leverages 400+ reactor-years of global liquid metal fast reactor operating history, validated at Idaho National Laboratory. The metallic-fuel, sodium-cooled fast spectrum design enables passive safety, compact footprint, and fuel flexibility that light-water reactor (LWR) designs cannot replicate. NuScale's LWR-based SMR does not compete in the same fuel cycle.
2. **Integrated Fuel Cycle Control:** Oklo's ability to recycle spent fuel and consume downblended government plutonium stockpiles creates a long-term structural cost advantage over HALEU-dependent competitors. Centrus Energy LOI (June 2026) for multi-year HALEU supply to the Ohio campus demonstrates active fuel supply chain development.
3. **Regulatory Path Differentiation:** The NRC's approval of Oklo's Principal Design Criteria Topical Report (on accelerated timeline — 15-day acceptance vs. standard 30–60 days) is a meaningful signal. The Part 57 regulatory framework, if codified, could reduce fleet licensing time from years to months per unit. Aurora-INL is progressing through DOE authorization rather than NRC (bypassing the framework that denied the first application in 2022), targeting late 2027 operation.
4. **Hyperscaler Demand Lock-In (Pre-Commercial):** 14 GW pipeline with Switch (12 GW non-binding MPA), Meta (1.2 GW campus, Ohio, with prepayment mechanism — partially binding), Equinix (500 MW LOI), and government/industrial customers. While mostly non-binding, the prepayment component of the Meta deal is a material differentiator — it implies customer willingness to put capital at risk before reactor operation.
5. **Sam Altman's Network:** While Sam Altman stepped down as chair in April 2025 (to avoid conflicts with OpenAI's power procurement), his network effect in placing Oklo inside the AI infrastructure conversation is non-trivial. NVIDIA collaboration (AI-driven fuel modeling with Los Alamos) further embeds Oklo in the AI-nuclear infrastructure narrative.

**Switching Costs:** Low pre-commercial. Once a hyperscaler co-locates a data center adjacent to an Oklo reactor campus, switching costs become very high (power infrastructure is fixed).

**The Consensus Mispricing — Core Thesis:**

The market is currently pricing OKLO as a binary lottery: either the reactors get built and generate extraordinary returns, or delays/regulatory failure destroys value. What the market underweights is the degree to which (a) the regulatory runway has structurally improved (DOE authorization path, accelerated NRC reviews, Part 57 framework), (b) the $2.54B cash position removes near-term existential risk, (c) the Meta prepayment structure de-risks the first commercial campus, and (d) Atomic Alchemy isotopes provide a bridge revenue pathway. The stock pulled back 70%+ from its October 2025 ATH of $193.84 — the current $61 level prices in a much more realistic, if still highly speculative, scenario set.

- **SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market):** ~$15–25 Billion | U.S.-focused AI data center and industrial advanced nuclear demand, where Oklo's regulatory standing, DOE relationships, and pipeline concentration exist.

- **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market):** ~$1–3 Billion annually by 2030–2032 | Based on realistic deployment of first 1.2 GW Ohio campus + Aurora-INL demonstration unit. At 75 MW per reactor, the Meta/Ohio campus alone represents 16 reactors at potentially $100–150/MWh under long-term PPA — implying $100–165M revenue per 75MW reactor annually at 90% capacity factor. The 1.2 GW campus could generate $1.6–2.2B in annual revenue at full build-out (by ~2034).

- **Part 57 Regulatory Framework:** If the NRC codifies the new Part 57 licensing framework for advanced reactors, Oklo's fleet deployment timeline could compress materially — reducing per-unit licensing burden.
- **Ohio Campus Financing / Binding Agreements:** Any conversion of Meta's prepayment arrangement into a fully binding offtake agreement, or DOE loan guarantee award, would be a material re-rating catalyst.
- **HALEU Supply Chain Solidification:** Centrus LOI (June 2026) for HALEU fuel covering up to five Aurora powerhouses de-risks the Ohio deployment.
- **AI Power Demand Acceleration:** Each quarter that U.S. data center power demand outpaces grid capacity strengthens the commercial logic for on-site SMR deployment — a structural tailwind for all near-term operators.

**Negative Catalysts (Headwinds):**

- **Continued ATM Dilution:** Oklo filed a fresh $1.0 billion ATM shelf in May 2026 — the second large ATM program in 12 months (following the $1.5B ATM in Dec 2025 that resulted in $1.18B raised). Each issuance compresses per-share value. Trailing 12-month dilution approximates 28%.
- **Aurora-INL Timeline Slippage:** Any delay past late-2027 pushes first revenue into 2028–2029 and stretches the investment thesis.
- **NRC Licensing Risk (Commercial Fleet):** The DOE authorization path applies to Aurora-INL only. Commercial fleet licensing still requires full NRC COLA approval — the same process that failed in 2022 (application denied without prejudice). Material execution risk.
- **HALEU Supply Chain Bottleneck:** Only Centrus commercially enriches HALEU in the U.S. A single-source dependency is a systemic supply risk.
- **Non-Binding Pipeline Risk:** Of the 14 GW pipeline, only the Meta arrangement includes a prepayment mechanism. The remaining ~12.8 GW (Switch + Equinix + others) are non-binding LOIs with no committed capital.

### 7. Key Investment Risks & Counter-Thesis

**Company-Specific Risks:**

- **NRC Licensing Risk (Critical):** Oklo's original Aurora application was denied by the NRC in January 2022 due to insufficient safety analysis data. While regulatory frameworks have improved (accelerated review, Part 57 development), commercial fleet licensing remains unproven. Any repeat denial would be devastating to the stock.
- **Execution Risk — Construction:** Nuclear construction projects globally have experienced systematic cost overruns and schedule delays (Vogtle Units 3 & 4 cost ~$35B vs. ~$14B estimate). Oklo is building first-of-a-kind commercial reactors. FOAK cost and schedule risk is structurally elevated. The $60M per 15MW reactor target is largely unvalidated.
- **HALEU Supply Chain Concentration:** The U.S. has a single commercial HALEU enricher (Centrus). Any enrichment capacity shortage, regulatory hold, or geopolitical interruption directly threatens Oklo's fuel supply chain.
- **Dilution:** Oklo has raised capital aggressively. The $1.5B ATM (Dec 2025) and $1.0B ATM (May 2026) represent serial dilution. Share count grew from ~157M (Dec 2025) to ~174M (June 2026) — a 10.8% dilution in six months. With SBC running at ~$62M annualized, and further ATM draws likely before first revenue, dilution is a persistent headwind.
- **Customer Pipeline Optionality (Non-Binding):** The 14 GW pipeline is not contracted revenue. Switch's 12 GW MPA is explicitly non-binding. If AI capital expenditure cycles retrench, or hyperscalers find alternative power solutions, the pipeline can evaporate without financial consequence to the counterparty.
- **Revenue Concentration Risk:** Even post-revenue, Oklo's customer base will likely be highly concentrated in 2–3 hyperscalers (Meta, Switch, Equinix) for the first several years of operation. Any single counterparty decision to delay or cancel materially impairs projections.

**Macroeconomic & Systemic Risks:**

- **Interest Rate Sensitivity:** As a zero-revenue, long-duration asset, OKLO's intrinsic value is highly sensitive to discount rates. A sustained higher-for-longer rate environment compresses the present value of 2030+ cash flows substantially.
- **Political/Policy Risk:** Nuclear energy licensing and DOE support are dependent on continued political will. Any reversion of federal nuclear policy, reduction in DOE site access, or NRC budget constraints could delay commercial licensing timelines.
- **Competition from Alternative Power Sources:** Grid-scale battery storage, geothermal, and natural gas with carbon capture are improving rapidly. If alternative reliable power solutions scale faster than Oklo's commercial deployment, hyperscalers may not need co-located SMRs.
- **Competing Nuclear Technologies:** TerraPower (Natrium reactor, backed by Bill Gates), X-energy, Kairos Power (Hermes reactor, Google PPA), and Commonwealth Fusion Energy are all competing for the same hyperscaler demand. TerraPower's Natrium reactor is scheduled to come online in 2030 in Wyoming; Google's Kairos deal (500MW, 2030+) demonstrates that Oklo is not the only horse in the race.
