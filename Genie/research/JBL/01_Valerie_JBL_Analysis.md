### Executive Summary & Investment Thesis

Jabil Inc. (NYSE: JBL) has successfully executed one of the most remarkable corporate transformations in the Electronic Manufacturing Services (EMS) sector. Historically pigeonholed as a low-margin consumer electronics assembler heavily reliant on Apple Inc., Jabil has aggressively pivoted. By selling its lower-margin **Mobility** business in early 2024 to BYD Electronic for \~$2.2 Billion and acquiring thermal specialist **Mikros Technologies**, Jabil has transitioned into a high-value, "silicon-to-systems" co-engineering partner for hyperscalers and AI data center operators.

Today, Jabil stands at the epicenter of the AI hardware build-out. Its **Intelligent Infrastructure** segment grew a staggering **+52% YoY** in Q2 FY2026 to reach $4.0 Billion—now constituting nearly half of the firm's total net revenue. 

However, at \~$365.00, the stock has experienced massive multiple expansion, pushing it past Wall Street's consensus average target ($303 - $320) and leaving virtually no margin of safety. This audit performs a rigorous fundamental dissection, balance sheet audit, and a mandatory **Reverse DCF Analysis** to determine the implied growth rates baked into this current valuation. Finally, we execute our proprietary **3-to-1 Asymmetric Upside Check** to establish a mathematically sound entry price.

### 1. Business Model & Moat Analysis

Historically, EMS companies operated on razor-thin operating margins (2% to 3%) with virtually zero pricing power. Jabil's transition to the **Intelligent Infrastructure** segment has structurally altered its margin profile and expanded its competitive moat:

*   **Silicon-to-Systems Co-Engineering Moat:** Jabil is no longer a simple contract manufacturer. It actively co-designs architectures from the silicon photonics level (such as 800G and 1.6T high-speed optical transceivers and co-packaged optics) up to full-rack system integration (compute, storage, and power).
*   **Proprietary Thermal Management Moat:** High-density AI server racks generate immense heat. Through the acquisition of **Mikros Technologies**, Jabil possesses specialized, proprietary micro-channel liquid cooling technology and advanced liquid-cooled server rack architectures. This engineering expertise creates a massive barrier to entry that traditional EMS peers cannot easily replicate.
*   **High Switching Costs & Operational Moats:** Jabil works in direct strategic partnerships with the world's leading cloud hyperscalers. Once a customized manufacturing line is co-developed, qualified, and integrated into a hyperscaler’s supply chain, the cost and operational risk of switching to a competitor are prohibitively high.
*   **Scale and Global Footprint:** Jabil’s ongoing $500 Million expansion of its U.S. manufacturing footprint (including a massive new facility in North Carolina operational by mid-2026) scales its capacity to meet the unprecedented demands of the AI data center build-out.

### 3. Valuation & Mandatory Reverse DCF Analysis

To evaluate the current stock price of **$365.00**, we execute a rigorous **Reverse Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)** model. Rather than guessing future growth rates, we mathematically solve for the exact 10-year annual FCF growth rate the market is baking in under standard assumptions:
*   **WACC ($r$):** 10.0%
*   **Terminal Growth Rate ($g$):** 3.0%
*   **Fully Diluted Shares Outstanding:** 105.6 Million
*   **Target Equity Value:** $38.544 Billion ($365.00 * 105.6M)
*   **Target Enterprise Value (for Unlevered DCF):** $40.590 Billion ($38.544B Equity + $2.046B Net Debt)

We present two standard methodologies to provide complete mathematical rigor.

#### Method A: Simplified Equity Value Reverse DCF
*Targeting current Equity Value ($38.544B). This treats FCF as Free Cash Flow to Equity (FCFE) and discounts directly to equity.*

$$\text{Equity Value} = \sum_{t=1}^{10} \frac{FCF_0 \times (1 + g_{\text{implied}})^t}{(1 + WACC)^t} + \frac{FCF_0 \times (1 + g_{\text{implied}})^{10} \times (1 + g_{\text{terminal}})}{(WACC - g_{\text{terminal}}) \times (1 + WACC)^{10}}$$

#### Method B: Unlevered Enterprise Value Reverse DCF
*Targeting Enterprise Value ($40.590B). This treats FCF as Free Cash Flow to Firm (FCFF) and factors in Jabil's Q2 FY2026 Net Debt ($2.046B).*

$$\text{Enterprise Value} = \text{Equity Value} + \text{Net Debt} = \sum_{t=1}^{10} \frac{FCF_0 \times (1 + g_{\text{implied}})^t}{(1 + WACC)^t} + \dots$$

#### Implied 10-Year Annual FCF Growth Rate Sensitivity Matrix

| Starting FCF Base ($FCF_0$) | Implied Growth Rate (Method A - Equity targeted) | Implied Growth Rate (Method B - EV targeted) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |

| **$1.2 Billion** (Normalized Base) | **13.55%** | **14.22%** |

#### Valuation Synthesis & Comparison
*   **Historical Growth:** Jabil's historical FCF CAGR over the last four years (FY2021 to FY2025) is approximately **19.8%** ($0.640B in FY21 to $1.318B in FY25).
*   **Is the Stock Overvalued at $365.00?**
    If we assume a normalized starting FCF base of **$1.2 Billion** (highly reasonable given Jabil's FY2025 FCF of $1.318B), the market is pricing in an implied growth rate of **13.55% to 14.22%** for the next 10 years.
    While Jabil has historically grown faster (19.8%), maintaining a \~14% compound growth rate for an entire decade in the highly cyclical hardware manufacturing industry is extremely difficult. Any "AI CapEx fatigue" or deceleration in hyperscaler spending would result in massive multiple contraction.
    Jabil’s trailing P/E has historically sat in the 10x-15x range. At $365.00, it is valued at **\~30x Core FY2026 EPS Guidance ($12.25)**, representing massive premium multiple expansion. We conclude that **Jabil is technically overvalued at $365.00**, as it leaves no room for execution errors or cyclical downturns.

### 5. Key Risks & Structural Vulnerabilities

While the transition to Intelligent Infrastructure has expanded its moat, Jabil is not immune to structural risks:

*   **Operating Margin Volatility:** Operating margins remain low (\~4.5% to 5.3%). In a high-volume, low-margin environment, minor cost overruns, yield inefficiencies, or inventory write-offs can completely wipe out net profitability.
*   **Hyperscaler Over-CapEx / Cyclical Fatigue:** If hyperscalers face pressure from investors to show monetization on their massive AI investments and choose to pause or slow down infrastructure build-outs, Jabil's high-capacity lines will sit idle, leading to severe operating deleverage.
*   **High Customer Concentration:** Despite divesting its Mobility business, Jabil's top five customers still represent **36%** of net revenue. Crucially, **Customer A represents 16% of total net revenue**, with sales heavily concentrated in the Intelligent Infrastructure segment. A slowdown from Customer A represents a devastating single-point-of-failure risk.
*   **Capital Reinvestment Risk:** Shifting to advanced technologies like co-packaged optics and liquid cooling requires continuous, high-intensity CapEx. The risk of over-building capacity during a cyclical peak is highly elevated.

### 7. Proprietary 3-to-1 Asymmetric Upside Audit

We apply our strict mathematical hurdle to determine if a buy-in at **$365.00** offers an asymmetric margin of safety.

*   **Bear Case Target (Downside):** **$230.00**  
    *Assumes a cyclical slowdown in AI infrastructure spending, operating margin contraction to 3.5%, and multiple compression back to a historical forward P/E of 18x on core EPS.*
*   **Bull Case Target (Upside):** **$450.00**  
    *Assumes flawless execution of the North Carolina plant, Intelligent Infrastructure maintaining >25% growth over 3 years, margins expanding to 6.0%, and FCF reaching $1.8B under a sustained 25x multiple.*

#### Payoff Ratio Calculation at $365.00 Entry:
$$\text{Upside per Share} = \$450.00 - \$365.00 = \$85.00$$
$$\text{Downside per Share} = \$365.00 - \$230.00 = \$135.00$$
$$\text{Payoff Ratio} = \frac{\$85.00}{\$135.00} = \mathbf{0.63x}$$

> [!WARNING]
> The payoff ratio at $365.00 is **0.63x**, failing our mandatory **3-to-1 asymmetric upside check** ($3.0\text{x}$ minimum). The risk-reward is heavily skewed to the downside.

#### Calculating the Maximum Buy-In Entry Price:
To satisfy our strict 3-to-1 asymmetric rule, the entry price ($P$) must satisfy:
$$\text{Upside} \ge 3 \times \text{Downside}$$
$$(450 - P) \ge 3 \times (P - 230)$$
$$450 - P \ge 3P - 690$$
$$4P \le 1140$$
$$P \le \$285.00$$

> [!IMPORTANT]
> To establish a true quantitative margin of safety, our maximum buy-in entry price for Jabil is **$285.00**. At this level, the 3-to-1 payoff ratio is fully restored. We recommend a **HOLD** or **Accumulate Only on Deep Pullbacks** below $285.00.
