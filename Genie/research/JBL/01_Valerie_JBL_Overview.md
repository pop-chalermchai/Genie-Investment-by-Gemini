### 🎯 3. Business Model & Economic Moat

* **Revenue Architecture (How it makes money):**
    * Jabil operates globally as a massive Electronic Manufacturing Services (EMS) and solutions provider. Revenue is highly diversified but increasingly driven by the IT & Cloud infrastructure segment (AI servers, networking) which is expected to account for $13.6B in FY2026. Other major segments include Automotive, Healthcare, and Packaging.
* **Competitive Advantage (Economic Moat Analysis):**
    * **Moat: Narrow but Deepening.** Source: *High Switching Costs & Scale Advantages.* Integrating deeply into the supply chains of tier-1 tech giants (like Apple and major hyperscalers) creates extremely sticky relationships. Migrating manufacturing to another partner introduces unacceptable supply chain risks and setup costs.
* **The Consensus Mispricing (The Core Thesis):**
    * The market historically priced JBL as a low-margin commodity manufacturer. The mispricing lies in underestimating the margin expansion and structural durability of its AI server manufacturing business, which acts as a permanent tailwind rather than a cyclical spike.

### 🛑 7. Key Investment Risks & Counter-Thesis

* **Company-Specific Risks:**
    * **Customer Concentration:** Apple has historically been a massive client. Any shift in supply chain strategy (e.g., in-sourcing or shifting to competitors like Foxconn/Luxshare) poses a material threat.
    * **Margin Ceiling:** EMS is notoriously low-margin. If AI server assembly commoditizes, Jabil's premium valuation will aggressively compress.
* **Macroeconomic & Systematic Risks:**
    * Geopolitical risks given heavy manufacturing presence in Asia (though Jabil is actively diversifying to Mexico and Eastern Europe).
