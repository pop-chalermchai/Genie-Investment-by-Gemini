### 🎯 3. Business Model & Economic Moat

* **Revenue Architecture (How it makes money):**
    * Amkor is the pioneer and second-largest player in the Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test (OSAT) market.
    * **Advanced Packaging (~70% of Revenue):** Includes flip-chip, wafer-level packaging, System-in-Package (SiP), and 2.5D/3D chiplet integration. Driven by AI accelerators, high-performance computing, and premium smartphones.
    * **Mainstream Packaging (~30% of Revenue):** Wirebonding and leadframe packaging, serving highly cyclical automotive, industrial, and consumer IoT applications.
* **Competitive Advantage (Economic Moat Analysis):**
    * **Moat Rating: Narrow but deepening.**
    * **Sources:** High Switching Costs and Technological Complexity. Advanced packaging is no longer a commoditized post-wafer service; it is an extension of front-end semiconductor manufacturing. Designing a chiplet-based processor requires co-developing high-density silicon interposers with foundries (like TSMC) and chip designers. Once a customer’s package architecture is qualified on Amkor’s lines, switching to another OSAT creates immense execution risk and months of requalification delays.
* **The Consensus Mispricing (The Core Thesis):**
    * The broader market treats Amkor as a low-margin, capital-intensive cyclical service business, penalizing the stock for its heavy $2.5B - $3.0B FY2026 CapEx budget.
    * This overlooks the fundamental structural pivot: as physical transistor scaling (Moore’s Law) slows, advanced packaging (chiplets) is the *only* viable path to increase chip performance.
    * Amkor's new Peoria, Arizona advanced packaging campus (opening in early 2028 in partnership with TSMC) will be the only high-volume packaging site in the U.S. By co-locating with TSMC's Arizona fab, Amkor secures a virtual monopoly on domestic, U.S.-sourced high-performance packaging. This positioning will command high-margin premiums and unlock massive multi-year FCF scaling.

### 🛑 7. Key Investment Risks & Counter-Thesis

* **Company-Specific Risks:**
    * **Customer Concentration:** Amkor's top client (traditionally Apple) represents a significant portion of net sales. Product cycle shifts or customer diversification away from Amkor could negatively impact revenue.
    * **Execution Risk in Arizona:** Developing a greenfield advanced packaging campus in the US faces higher construction costs, potential labor shortages for specialized microelectronics technicians, and operational integration hurdles with TSMC.
* **Macroeconomic & Systematic Risks:**
    * **Geopolitical Exposure:** Over 85% of Amkor's manufacturing footprint is located in East Asia (South Korea, Philippines, Japan, China, Taiwan, Vietnam). Any escalation of geopolitical tensions in the Taiwan Strait or the Korean Peninsula represents a catastrophic risk to global supply chains.
