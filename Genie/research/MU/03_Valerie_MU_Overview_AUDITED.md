### 🎯 3. Business Model & Economic Moat

* **Revenue Architecture (How it makes money):**
    * Micron derives revenue primarily from the sale of DRAM and NAND memory chips. Currently, the most lucrative and fastest-growing segment is High Bandwidth Memory (HBM3E and beyond) sold directly to hyperscalers and GPU manufacturers (e.g., NVIDIA, AMD) for AI data center infrastructure.
* **Competitive Advantage (Economic Moat Analysis):**
    * **Moat: Narrow to Wide (Evolving).** Historically a commoditized industry with no moat, the oligopoly (Micron, Samsung, SK Hynix) has shown severe capacity constraints. The technical difficulty of manufacturing advanced HBM has created high barriers to entry and strong pricing power, effectively widening Micron's moat during this AI supercycle.
* **The Consensus Mispricing (The Core Thesis):**
    * The market is pricing Micron as a high-margin software/SaaS business due to 80%+ gross margins. The core risk is that the market assumes these margins and HBM supply shortages will last into perpetuity, ignoring the cyclical nature of semiconductor capital expenditures and capacity expansion by competitors.

### 🛑 7. Key Investment Risks & Counter-Thesis

* **Company-Specific Risks:**
    * **Cyclicality Masked as Secular Growth:** AI memory demand is unprecedented, but capital intensity is high. If hyperscalers pause infrastructure build-outs, Micron will be left with massive unutilized capacity and crashing margins.
* **Macroeconomic & Systematic Risks:**
    * **Geopolitics:** Export restrictions on AI technologies to certain regions limit the ultimate TAM. Furthermore, the supply chain heavily relies on East Asian stability.
